## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Genuinely product-specific where it matters, generic in the chrome around it. The core mechanism — one persistent set of 7 GLB meshes that assemble/explode/reassemble as the single hero object across all 7 sections — is built entirely around this burger's real stack order and the fire-cooking claim. Section 03 (Fire/Patty) is where direction, mechanism, and product truth converge: macro camera on the patty, dedicated grill-mark fade-in, 24 individually-phased embers with sinusoidal flicker tied to scroll. That section earns its differentiation claim. The surrounding system — schematic-grid background, mono kicker format, exploded-diagram leader lines — is a known "technical parts-diagram" genre that could be reskinned onto any product with interchangeable parts without touching layout logic.

**Deterministic scan**: Static scan of `app`/`components` (exit 0): 1 advisory finding, `codex-grid-background` (`globals.css:52`) — likely a false positive here, since the grid overlay is the direction contract's own committed "exploded-diagram" motif, not a generic template artifact. Full-render URL scan of localhost:3000 (exit 2, 25 findings): `low-contrast` ×11, `undersized-ui-text` ×7, `wide-tracking` ×3, `kicker-above-heading` ×2, `hero-eyebrow-chip` ×1, `tiny-text` ×1, `em-dash-overuse` ×1 (advisory). The browser-injected live overlay independently corroborated the 12 non-contrast findings.

Two points of real agreement between the assessments, reached independently:
- **The 7 `undersized-ui-text` findings are the exact same elements** the LLM review flagged from a different angle (low contrast) under the Sam persona: ingredient sub-labels ("TOASTED BRIOCHE" ×2, "2X AGED CHEDDAR", "180G — 100% BEEF", "FLAME-KISSED ROMAINE", "VINE-RIPENED", "EMBER GLAZE") are simultaneously too small (10px) and too low-contrast. Two different methods, same elements — this label system needs a real pass, not a tweak.
- **The `low-contrast` findings on "BURGER," "CHEESE," "PATTY," "LETTUCE"** (several at a suspicious, degenerate exact 1.0:1 ratio) point at the same region the LLM review independently named its #1 priority issue (the 3D burger reading as near-invisible against the iron background). The detector's precise ratios are unreliable here — DOM text sampling can't see through to what the WebGL canvas actually renders behind it, which is very likely why several unrelated elements all compute to an identical 1.0:1 — but two independent methods converging on "this whole visual field is hard to see" is real signal even where the exact numbers aren't trustworthy.

The `kicker-above-heading` / `hero-eyebrow-chip` / `wide-tracking` findings are a genre-pattern the detector flags mechanically; given the locked direction contract explicitly commits to this kicker/tracking system as part of "Graphic & Technical," these read as by-design rather than accidental genericism and are not elevated to priority issues below.

**Visual overlays**: The console overlay was captured during Assessment B (`[impeccable] 12 anti-patterns found`, matching the non-contrast findings above), but the tab and its local visualization server were already closed as part of required cleanup before this report — there is no live `[Human]` tab open right now to view; the findings above are the full extracted record of what it showed.

## Overall Impression

The one thing this site had to prove — that the burger is cooked over live fire, not a flat-top — it proves better than almost anything else on the page: Section 03 is genuinely excellent, specific, and appetizing. The rest of the site is a well-built, well-architected technical achievement (a single synchronized 3D transform system driving 7 sections, thoughtful reduced-motion handling) let down by a legibility problem that runs through nearly every section: the hero object is hard to see against its own background, the busiest section on the page (Quality/Numbers) is also the hardest to read, and the smallest text (ingredient specs) is both undersized and low-contrast by two independent measurements. The biggest opportunity is a dedicated legibility/lighting pass on the burger itself and the type-sandwich section — the mechanism and the craft are already there.

## What's Working

1. **Section 03 (Fire/Patty)** is the standout: the camera macros onto the patty, grill marks fade in via a dedicated component, and 24 individually-phased embers flicker with scroll-tied sinusoidal emissive intensity — this is the one place the "live-fire, not a flat-top" claim is *shown*, not stated.
2. **`prefers-reduced-motion` handling is unusually considered.** The code specifically strips ambient idle drift and pointer-tied tilt while deliberately preserving the scroll-phase transitions that carry meaning — a real distinction between decorative and meaning-carrying motion that most sites collapse into "kill everything."
3. **Zero JS runtime errors** across a full scroll sweep and interaction pass (confirmed independently by the detector's browser evidence), and the single-source-of-truth transform architecture keeps two separate canvases (the main rig and the front-layer cheese canvas) perfectly in sync, with a deliberately asymmetric damping curve so the final reassembly reads as a landing, not another hover response.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The signature 3D burger is nearly invisible against its own background in the Hero.**
*Why it matters*: The brief states this 3D object *is* the central differentiation mechanism. If a visitor doesn't register it in the first few seconds, they've gotten less than a static photo would have given them. Independently corroborated: the detector's `low-contrast` findings landed on exactly this region (BURGER/CHEESE/PATTY/LETTUCE), even though its precise ratios are unreliable this close to a WebGL canvas.
*Fix*: A dedicated rim-light/key-light pass tuned per-phase for legibility against the iron palette in the Hero framing specifically, not just the Fire section's tighter macro lighting.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable polish` (or `/impeccable typeset`/`colorize` if scoped narrowly to the lighting/contrast pass)

**[P1] Section 05's typography/mesh "sandwich" collides with itself and bleeds into the Section 06 CTA.**
*Why it matters*: This is the loudest moment on the page and also the hardest to read — stat type runs directly through ingredient meshes, and at points the front-copy overlay is still at full opacity across "VIEW THE FULL MENU," cutting through the one button on the site.
*Fix*: Shorten the overlay's visible window or add a fade keyed to scroll distance from the CTA so type clears before the button becomes interactive.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] On mobile, the Ingredients-section label grid visually collides with the burger mesh.**
*Why it matters*: Confirmed at a 390px viewport — "CHEESE / 2X AGED CHEDDAR" sits directly across the tomato disc. Mobile is the primary use context per the brief. Independently corroborated: these are the exact same labels the detector flagged as `undersized-ui-text` (10px) — two methods, same elements.
*Fix*: At narrow viewports, either reposition the assembled burger to clear the label grid, or delay the label grid's fade-in until the explode has separated the pieces.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] The Build section's advertised tilt interaction is silently unreachable on iPhone.**
*Why it matters*: iOS Safari requires an explicit `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()` call behind a user gesture before `deviceorientation` will ever fire; the code never calls it. Tilting the phone in response to "MOVE UP AND DOWN" does nothing — instead an unrelated idle sine-drift silently substitutes, which can read as "it's responding to me" when it isn't.
*Fix*: Gate tilt behind an explicit permission-request tap with copy explaining why, and give touch users a real alternative (vertical drag/scrub) rather than a silent autoplay stand-in.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P3] Global `user-select: none` will block copying the phone number and address once real content lands.**
*Why it matters*: Low-impact today since Footer content is placeholder, but the two fields a deciding mobile visitor most wants to copy into Maps/Phone are currently unselectable by design, sitewide.
*Fix*: Scope `user-select: none` to the decorative/3D layers only; leave Footer contact text selectable, and add `tel:`/maps `href`s once real numbers exist.
*Suggested command*: `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer, deciding whether to visit)**: Sees "MOVE TO SEPARATE" pointing at a burger that's nearly invisible against the background — the instruction has no visible target for several seconds. Scrolls through Ingredients and hits a long dead zone (~85% of that section's scroll) before anything visibly changes, right after a strong Hero. Follows "VIEW THE FULL MENU" specifically for pricing/menu signal and lands on 4 placeholder lines and "coming soon" — honest, but the CTA's own wording over-promises relative to what it delivers, so the one action the whole page builds toward doesn't move Jordan's decision forward.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: Same near-invisible Hero burger, worse on a dim phone screen glanced at quickly. Ingredients-section text/mesh collision reads as clutter on a fast skim. If interrupted mid-scroll on a 9500px+ page, the only "where am I" cue is a small corner kicker. Footer's placeholder phone/address is neither a link nor selectable — nothing currently supports "get me there / call them," which is the site's stated job.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent)**: The single densest content moment on the page — "180G / 100% BEEF / OPEN FIRE / 2X CHEESE" — is `aria-hidden` on both its back and front copies with no accessible equivalent anywhere else; a screen reader gets only the "05 — QUALITY / NUMBERS" kicker for that entire section, which also has no heading element to navigate to. The whole homepage has exactly one focusable interactive element; a keyboard user tabs past all seven sections to reach it, with no skip link. Several secondary text treatments compute to roughly 2.4:1 contrast, well under WCAG AA's 4.5:1. Credit where due: `prefers-reduced-motion` is handled better here than on most sites.

## Minor Observations

- Only one `<a>` element exists on the entire homepage; the "BURGER EMBER" wordmark isn't a link back to top.
- Visitor-facing copy leaks internal spec names: "BUILD," "MAGNETIC SPACING — POINTER CONTROLLED," "GRAPHIC & TECHNICAL" (footer tagline) read like direction-system labels, not customer copy.
- Ember-orange marks both the CTA border (interactive) and non-interactive kicker numbers/hover text, diluting "orange = clickable" as a learned signal.
- No visible loading/skeleton state observed for the WebGL canvas or GLB fetch — untested on a throttled connection, and there's no code path handling a slow/failed load.
- `em-dash-overuse` (8 instances, advisory) — worth a copy pass regardless of severity.
- `wide-tracking` (3 findings) and the kicker/eyebrow pattern read as intentional given the locked "Graphic & Technical" direction — flagged by the detector, but not treated as issues here.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the 3D burger is the whole point, should its lighting be authored *per phase* specifically for legibility against the iron palette, rather than one static three-light rig used everywhere?
2. Ingredients spends most of its scroll before anything visibly moves — what if the explode mapping front-loaded, so the "seven layers, nothing hidden" payoff starts on the first scroll tick instead of the last 15%?
3. Given the primary persona decides on mobile, and Build's real interaction (device tilt) is gated behind a permission prompt the code never triggers, should "pointer-controlled magnetic spacing" be redesigned mobile-first around a drag/scrub gesture instead of a ported mouse metaphor?
4. Is Section 05's type/mesh "sandwich" worth its legibility cost — would the stats and the burger tell a stronger story taking turns for attention rather than occupying the same pixels at once?
